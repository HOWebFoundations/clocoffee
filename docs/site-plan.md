## 1. Framing: Higgsfield is the asset factory, Next.js is the product

[Certain] The Higgsfield workspace is at 0 credits on the free plan. "Everything generated by Higgsfield"
is therefore not a production plan — it is a top-up decision that has not been made.

And even funded, Higgsfield cannot generate a website component. It generates pixels: stills, video, speech,
GLB meshes. Everything that will make this site feel *super interactive* — a pour that scrubs with scroll, a cup
you can spin, a drink builder that ends in an order — is code, and the quality of that code is what separates
this from every café template on Earth.

The all-AI framing fails for a second reason. [Likely] The three painted cups physically exist in the shop.
A phone on a tripod, a color-matched poster board and a cheap turntable will give truer 360 spins and menu
photos than any image model, for zero credits — and locals can tell when a café has no real photo of itself.

**The approach instead:** spend credits only on what a camera cannot do (soda-commercial pours, the character
reviewer, cinemagraphs, cutouts, upscales) and shoot the rest in one afternoon.

**The risk being avoided:** a site that reads as an AI demo, costs a credit pack deliberately dropped in July,
and still shows nobody where the shop is.

## 3. Positioning and assumptions

[Guessing] CloCoffee is a single-location shop in Lebanon whose customers arrive from Instagram on a phone, and
the cups are also sold as merchandise. **If either is wrong, sections 4 and 5 change shape.**

The site has one job: turn a scroll into a WhatsApp order or a visit, and make the painted cups the thing people
remember. Everything interactive must serve that, or it is decoration that slows the page.

### Creative concept: "Three cups, three worlds"

The cream-and-espresso base of the logo is the neutral stage. Each cup owns a color world — iris blue-violet with
green and lemon yellow; blossom pink/magenta; ditsy multicolor — that takes over the screen when that cup is
active. The bloom from the logo becomes the site's transition motif.

- [Certain] The supplied assets are raster throughout. The wordmark lettering still needs vectorizing before
  favicons, signage and print. Keep the bloom as an alpha PNG — and cut it off the opaque cream circle it
  currently sits on, or it will not composite over dark sections.
- [Certain] Espresso on cream measures ~11:1 in both directions — AAA, and unusually strong for a warm
  palette. The site can run that pair everywhere with no accessibility tension.
- [Certain] Blush fails on cream (~1.4:1) but is AAA on espresso (~8:1). The brass-gold trap from
  thehowf.com applies to cream grounds and *inverts* on dark ones: blush display type on an espresso
  section is available and looks deliberate. Mid violet fails on cream (~2.9:1); deep violet clears AA by
  0.02, which is a rounding error rather than a margin.
- Ratios above are computed from eyeballed hexes. Re-sample from source files before they reach code —
  [`brand.md`](brand.md) §2 carries the values and the caveat.

## 4. Section map and interaction spec

| # | Section | Interaction | Assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Bloom intro | 600 ms watercolor bloom reveal, first visit only, skippable | bloom PNG |
| 1 | Hero: The Pour | Hero loop plays muted behind the wordmark (poster image first). Scrolling scrubs a frame sequence of the cup filling; the headline rises with the coffee. Two CTAs: Order on WhatsApp, See the menu | hero loop, pour frames |
| 2 | Three cups, three worlds | Pinned horizontal section on desktop, swipe cards on mobile. Background tints to the active world. Cup cutout floats with mouse parallax (device tilt opt-in). Drag to rotate (360 frames). Tap to pour (plays that cup's Seedance fill) | cutouts, 360 frames, pour videos |
| 3 | Build your drink | Four steps: base (espresso, latte, iced, matcha), milk, sweetness, cup. Live preview swaps precomposed stills. Price in LBP and USD. Ends in "Send to WhatsApp" with the order text prefilled, plus "Save as image" | stills, cutouts |
| 4 | Menu | Filterable grid; hover lifts a card and tints the page to its world color. Content from Supabase so Chloe can edit prices without a developer | menu photos |
| 5 | The Verdict | Phone-framed 9:16 reels, sound on tap, rotating captions | reviewer clips |
| 6 | The shop | Real interior photos, open-now indicator computed from hours, map, directions, WhatsApp | photos — **not yet supplied**; the two storefront images on hand are concept renders of two different buildings and must not ship (`brand.md` §4) |
| 7 | Footer | Curated static 6-tile Instagram grid, hours, socials | — |

### Micro-interactions

- Cursor becomes a coffee ring over anything tappable (desktop only)
- Page transitions use the bloom as a mask via the View Transitions API, as progressive enhancement
- Buttons fill like liquid
- Café ambience sound toggle, armed only after a user gesture

Every one of these collapses to static posters and fades under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

### Ordering and social

- [Certain] The WhatsApp order is a plain `wa.me` deep link with prefilled text. No payment capture on the site.
- [Likely] Cash, Whish and OMT are how a Lebanese café actually gets paid; a card flow would be a liability for
  no revenue.
- [Certain] Instagram's Basic Display API is dead — the footer feed is curated images, not a live pull.

## 6. Stack and performance budget

[Certain] Next.js App Router on Vercel — the same pipeline as thehowf.com, built in Claude Code.

- TypeScript, Tailwind
- Motion for UI micro-interactions
- GSAP ScrollTrigger for the pinned and scrubbed sections
- Lenis for smooth scroll
- Supabase for menu data, with a protected admin route for Chloe
- [Certain] Video and frame sequences live on Cloudflare R2 (zero egress), **not** in the Vercel bundle where
  bandwidth is metered

Pour frames: roughly 120 WebP frames at 1280 px for desktop, 60 at 720 px for mobile, drawn to canvas, loaded
progressively. [Guessing] 4–6 MB desktop, under 2 MB mobile.

### Budget — non-negotiable

- LCP under 2.5 s on a mid-range Android over 4G
- Hero poster in AVIF before any video
- All motion and the spinner dynamically imported
- First-load JS under 200 KB gzipped
- Lighthouse mobile at 90 or better before launch

[Likely] Lebanese mobile networks will punish anything heavier, and most visitors open this inside the Instagram
in-app browser.

## 7. Conversion and search

[Likely] The highest-ROI item for a coffeeshop is the Google Business Profile, not the site. Set it up with the
hero still, hours and the WhatsApp number, and link the site from it.

On-site:

- `CafeOrCoffeeShop`, `Menu` and `MenuItem` schema; opening hours; geo
- English first, Arabic RTL second
- Plausible or Vercel Analytics, so there is no cookie banner
- The drink builder emits an analytics event per completed WhatsApp handoff — **that number is the only KPI that
  matters**
